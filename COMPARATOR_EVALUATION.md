## 1. What was implemented

| method | source | statistic | inference | implementation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Permutation null | the incumbent, `recompute/null_reference.py` | max over partitions of the within-partition max−min subgroup AUROC | stratified joint permutation, B=10,000, seed 42 | existing; results read back from `results/null_joint/` |
| DiCiccio et al. 2020 (KDD) | *Evaluating Fairness Using Permutation Tests* | max over admissible subgroup pairs of the **studentized** difference `|AUC_i − AUC_j| / sqrt(Var_i + Var_j)` | same stratified joint permutation, same seed, same B, single-step max-T | `recompute/comparators/diciccio.py` (ours; no published package implements it) |
| Lum, Zhang & Bower 2022 (FAccT) | *De-biasing "bias" measurement* | double-corrected variance `V_dc = S² − mean(v_k)` across subgroup AUROCs, per partition | closed-form z-test on `V_dc`; Cochran's Q and a parametric-bootstrap CI reported alongside | `recompute/comparators/lum.py` (ours; no published package implements it) |
| Four-fifths rule | 29 CFR 1607.4D | `min_k AUROC_k / max_k AUROC_k`, worst partition | none — deterministic screen at 0.80 | **fairlearn 0.13.0**, `MetricFrame.ratio(method="between_groups")` |
| Fixed threshold | current practice | the incumbent's point statistic | none — deterministic screen at 0.05 | `recompute/comparators/naive.py` |

`Var` is DeLong's structural-component variance throughout, checked against a
stratified bootstrap and against `roc_auc_score` to 1e−16.

### Packages

`fairlearn` 0.13.0 was already installed and is used for the four-fifths rule,
because `MetricFrame.ratio(method="between_groups")` is exactly that statistic.
Our loop-friendly equivalent is asserted equal to it on every cohort under every
inclusion rule (`test_four_fifths_matches_fairlearn_on_every_cohort`).

`aif360` 0.6.1 and `aequitas` 1.1.0 were checked with a dry-run install and **not
installed**. Both would downgrade `pandas` from 3.0.2 to 2.3.3 — the environment
the published null results were computed in — which is an unacceptable
reproducibility risk for numbers going to press. Neither adds anything here in
any case: their disparity modules compute ratios and differences that fairlearn
already provides, and neither implements DiCiccio's studentized permutation test
or Lum's double-corrected variance estimator. **Nothing was added to the
environment.**

### Adaptations, stated

*DiCiccio.* The paper tests one pair with a freely permuted group label. Two
changes. (i) The permutation is stratified within outcome class, holding every
subgroup's size and prevalence fixed — a free permutation would resample each
group's event count and leave AUROCs unestimable, and stratification is what
makes the draws identical to the incumbent's so the two differ in the statistic
alone. (ii) The two-group test is extended to many groups by single-step max-T
over all admissible pairs, sharing one permutation pass. max-T exploits the
dependence between pairs instead of paying Bonferroni for it; it is the
strongest valid combination, which is what the method is owed. Holm-adjusted
per-pair p-values, permutation and asymptotic, are in the CSV as a conservative
cross-check.

*Lum.* The paper's estimator is a corrected **variance** across groups; the
incumbent's statistic is a max−min **range**. `V_dc` is implemented literally,
with its bias derived in the module docstring from
`E[S²] = τ² + mean(v_k)`. The uncertainty quantification is re-derived in closed
form,

```
Var(V_dc) = 2/(L−1)² · [ (1 − 2/L)·Σσ_k⁴ + (Σσ_k²)²/L² ]
```

which collapses to the textbook `2σ⁴/(L−1)` when the variances are equal
(pinned by test). Because the range admits no comparably clean correction, two
range-scale analogues are reported next to the incumbent's Δ: `range_moment`
= `τ̂·d_L` with `d_L = E[range of L standard normals]`, and `range_shrunk`, the
range of the empirical-Bayes shrunken subgroup AUROCs. Three inferential readings
of the same estimator are reported, because they behave very differently and the
difference is itself a finding.

**The pattern is one-directional and it is not in the incumbent's favour.** At
`m30` the incumbent flags one cohort of nine. DiCiccio flags six and Lum's
z-test six, and **neither misses anything the incumbent finds**. The same holds
at every inclusion rule except `ev10`, where the incumbent picks up NHIS 2024
(DiCiccio p = 0.058, just outside) and NHIS 2023 (Lum's z-test p = 0.21).

**Why.** The mechanism is visible in Adult Income and is structural, not
incidental. Its three partitions give observed gaps of 0.058 (sex), 0.081 (race)
and 0.067 (age). The race partition contains levels of n = 80 with 9 events and
n = 90 with 13 events; the age partition is two levels of n = 3,989 and n = 5,056
carrying 1,430 and 812 events. The incumbent's statistic is the **maximum over
partitions of a raw range**, so it reports 0.081 from race — and its null is the
maximum over partitions too, so the same tiny race levels inflate the reference
distribution to a 95th percentile of 0.139. The 0.067 age gap, which is over
twelve standard errors and beyond serious doubt, is never tested on its own
terms: it is smaller than the race gap, so it never becomes the statistic, and
the null it would have been compared against was widened by a different
partition's noise. Lum's decomposition says the same thing arithmetically — for
Adult Income's age partition only 1.3% of the observed dispersion is sampling
noise.

Both comparators avoid this because both work on a noise-adjusted scale *within*
each comparison before any maximum is taken. Studentization divides each pair's
difference by its own standard error, so a noisy pair gets a large denominator
rather than a large numerator. The bias correction subtracts each partition's own
mean sampling variance. Neither lets one partition's imprecision set the
threshold for another partition's signal.

**Where they genuinely disagree with each other.** The two Lum readings split on
NHIS 2023 at `ev10`: the unweighted `V_dc` z-test gives p = 0.21 and does not
flag, Cochran's Q on the same inputs gives p = 3.8e−04 and does. `V_dc` weights
every subgroup equally, so a noisy level drags the estimate; Q weights by
precision and down-weights it. That is a real methodological difference, not a
bug, and it is why both are reported.

**The four-fifths rule barely engages.** It flags three cohorts at `m30`, and
inspection of the reason matters more than the count: the gap a cohort needs
before the rule can fire is `0.2 × max_k AUROC_k`, which is 0.157 to 0.191 across
these ten cohorts. NHIS 2023's 0.128 gap, ACS-Income's 0.053 gap, and Adult
Income's 0.081 gap are all structurally invisible to it. The rule was written for
selection rates, which have a meaningful zero; AUROC has an uninformative point
at 0.5, so a ratio of 0.80 corresponds to a subgroup performing at chance while
another performs at 0.63. `min_detectable_gap` is reported per partition so this
is visible rather than implied. The three cohorts it does flag are the three with
a subgroup AUROC near or below 0.6.

**The naive 0.05 threshold flags most things.** Seven of nine at `m30`. It is the
most sensitive rule in the table and it has no error control at all, which is
exactly why the Type I column below is the one that matters.
